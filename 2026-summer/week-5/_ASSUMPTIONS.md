## The dataset

- **Synthetic and deterministic.** Generated by `assets/build_week5_invoices.py`.
  Same seed gives byte-identical files. No real people or companies; all names are
  fictional business accounts.
- **Two separate sets.** The lab (seed 20260701) and the homework (seed 20260815)
  are generated independently, so a lab answer cannot be carried into the homework.
- **Files.** `invoices.csv` (one row per invoice: invoice_id, customer_id,
  invoice_date, amount, paid_date) and `customers.csv` (customer_id, customer_name,
  segment, net_terms). Students attach name and terms with XLOOKUP.

## Aging methodology: days past due (terms-equalized)

Net terms are mixed (Commercial 60/90/180, Consumer 30), so aging by days since the
invoice date would not be comparable across customers. A net-180 invoice at 130 days
outstanding is current; a net-30 invoice at 130 days is 100 days late. **Aging is
therefore measured in days past due**, against each invoice's own due date. This is
why the lab attaches `net_terms`: you need it to find the due date.

- **Snapshot (as-of) date: 2026-03-31.** Aging is measured against this date unless
  a different as-of date is chosen (the dashboard date selector).
- **Due date** = invoice_date + net_terms (the customer's terms).
- **Days past due** = (paid_date if paid, else the as-of date) minus the due date.
  A value of zero or less means the invoice is **not yet due** (within terms).
- **Aging buckets**, exact labels: **`Not due`** (within terms), then `0-30`,
  `31-60`, `61-90`, `91-120`, `121-150`, `151-180`, `181+`, all measured in days
  **past due**. Both paid and open invoices are bucketed (a paid invoice's bucket is
  how late it was paid).
- **Open vs paid.** A blank `paid_date` means the invoice is open as of the snapshot.
  A `paid_date` is recorded only if payment landed on or before the snapshot.
- **Paid in full.** Every invoice with a paid_date is paid in full. No partials.
- **Voids: none.** Every invoice is a live receivable, open or paid in full.
- **Late-payment trickle.** Most slow payers settle within ~90 days past due, but a
  small share of genuinely delinquent accounts pay far later. Well under 1% of
  payments land 181+ days past due (the latest land roughly 10 to 11 months past
  due; the exact share varies slightly by seed), so the high buckets see a
  realistic trickle of collections rather than a hard cliff.
- **Days to pay** (a separate metric, for the scatter and histogram) stays measured
  from the **invoice date**: paid_date minus invoice_date. It answers "how long did
  they take," which is different from "how late were they."

## Invoice cohort (vintage) view

The dashboard's cohort table groups invoices by **due month** (not billing month) so
that every invoice in a cohort comes due at the same time and ages in lockstep,
regardless of net terms. Following a fully-aged cohort, the share that reaches 181+
days past due and stays uncollected is a steady-state **bad-debt loss rate** (the
March 2025 due-cohort, for example, settles near 9% uncollectible). Recent cohorts
have not aged and understate the loss; average several mature cohorts for a reserve.

## Segments and terms

- **Commercial:** net 60, 90, or 180. Larger invoices, fewer of them, slower payers.
- **Consumer:** net 30. Smaller invoices, more of them, a mix of fast and slow.

## Filters

- The lab and the dashboard include **every invoice in the window** unless a chart
  says otherwise. Nothing is excluded by default.
- The one explicit subset is the lab's **January 2026 column chart**: only invoices
  with an invoice_date in 2026-01.
- The dashboard states its active as-of date, the days-past-due basis, and that
  voids are not present. The date selector recomputes aging as of any date.
